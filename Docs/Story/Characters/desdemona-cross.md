# Desdemona "Desi" Cross — Marcus's Wife (deceased)

Built from Orlando's story intake answers (2026-09-13). Voice notes are still open —
see `Docs/Story/Dialogue/dialogue-intake-form.md`, not yet answered. Since Desi only
appears via memory/dream sequences, her voice notes matter especially for how those
scenes are written and read.

## Role

Not a present-tense character — she appears through Marcus's memories, starting with
the opening dream sequence. She is the reason for everything Marcus does: Aegis-Corvus
Dynamics murdered her and stole her research, which is what put Marcus on this path in
the first place.

## Physical description

Beautiful, light-skinned woman with glasses and a warm smile — "the memory Marcus
holds onto since he can no longer see her with his own eyes." (Worth noting: this line
from the intake is doing real thematic work — Marcus's only remaining access to what
Desi looked like is memory, not his current cybernetic perception, which gives him
data rather than images even of the world in front of him now.)

## Background

A highly intelligent doctor, in charge of bio-mechanical molecular research — working
on cures for diseases like cancer and other life-threatening conditions. Top of her
field, well respected and revered by colleagues and peers. Aegis-Corvus stole this
research after her death (per the project's existing lore bible in `MEMORY.md`).

## Why she died — updated 2026-09-14

Her death is no longer a separate tragedy from the supernatural plot. Per the story
bible's "the bloodline is the bioweapon" section, **Aegis-Corvus's illegal bioweapons
research was studying the Cross bloodline**, which places Desi — a bio-mechanical
molecular researcher married to a Cross — at the exact point where the corporate crime
and the blood secret meet. That intersection is what the story's theme now turns on,
which makes her far more structurally load-bearing than "the murdered wife who
motivates the protagonist."

**Still open, and it changes how her scenes play:** did she *discover* what Marcus is,
or was she killed without ever knowing? If she knew, every memory scene carries a
second layer — she's looking at a husband whose nature she understands and he doesn't,
and the audience may be watching her decide whether to tell him. If she never knew,
the memories stay clean and the tragedy is simpler. The tightest version is that her
discovery is precisely what got her killed, but that hasn't been chosen yet (see the
story bible's open questions).
